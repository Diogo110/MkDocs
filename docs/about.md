# About me

![Diogo](assets/photos/me.jpg){ align=right width=220 }

Hi, I'm Diogo, a software developer from Vila Nova de Famalicão, Portugal. I'm
happiest when I'm learning something new, whether that's a language, a tool, or
how a game I love was built.

## What I do

I'm a **Software Developer at MVF Software**, where I work on gym and sports
management software. I started there in April 2025 as a six-month intern, and
in October 2025 I joined the team full time.

My work covers the whole stack: server modules and databases, REST APIs and
DataSnap services, data access with the Aurelius ORM, and the ViewModels and
interfaces on top. I also connect the software to third-party APIs. On the
team I help fix bugs and add new behaviour to the sports management system, and
I work with management to get changes into production.

Two things I'm proud of there, both done on my own:

- the **member self-service kiosk**, designed and built from scratch;
- the **integration with AI and machine learning** in the company's software.

I often start from the designer's prototypes, and sometimes I propose the UI
myself. I test my own work and ship new versions as the feedback comes in.

## How I got here

I began in **Digital Games Development Engineering** (2020 to 2021), then
switched to **Computer Systems Engineering** (2021 to 2024) at UPCA, the
Polytechnic University of Cávado and Ave, to learn a broader side of
technology: software development, databases, architecture and systems.

In 2024 I did an internship at **Edigma**, where I built a side-scrolling game
in Qt with QML and JavaScript, and a *Restart on Crash* tool in C++. While I
was studying I also worked part time at Ribapão, which helped me build
responsibility, teamwork and time management.

## Where I'm heading: back to games

Games are where I started, and I want to go back to them. I'm working on a
game of my own. Right now that means designing **levels**, writing the
**story**, coming up with ideas for new **mechanics** and **drawing** the
world and its characters.

I'm a playtester at heart, and that's where my passion for game development
started. Playtesting taught me to notice what feels good and what feels broken,
and to describe a problem clearly enough for a developer to fix it. Now I get
to be on the other side of the screen.
